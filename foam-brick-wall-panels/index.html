Choosing a pattern

  • Brick — the classic white brick for café, Scandinavian or industrial looks.
  • Wood — warm grain for natural interiors.
  • Stone & cement — stacked stone or exposed-concrete style.
  • Mono block & Mondrian — large geometric formats for a clean, modern wall.
  • Rolls & sheets — fewer seams across wide walls.

How many panels do you need?

One 77 × 71 cm panel covers about 0.55 m² (5.9 sq ft). Divide your wall area by 0.55 m² (or 5.9 sq ft) and add about 10% for cutting.

Installation tips

  1. Clean and dry the wall.
  2. Start the first panel level, from the ceiling line or the floor.
  3. Match the relief to the neighbouring panel as you peel the backing gradually.
  4. Press edges firmly with your palm and trim excess with a knife.

Precautions

  • For indoor use only — not for outdoor walls, showers or areas that get wet.
  • Keep away from open flames and heat sources such as heaters and stoves.
  • Will not bond well to heavily textured walls, water-repellent or flame-retardant coated wallpaper, or slick surfaces.
  • Removing the product may damage the wall surface underneath. Renters should check with the landlord first and test in an inconspicuous spot.
  • Keep small children from chewing or peeling the panels.

Frequently asked questions

How are foam brick panels different from regular wallpaper?

They are made of thick PE foam that physically reproduces the relief of brick or stone, so the wall looks three-dimensional and feels soft. They also take the cold feel off bare walls.

Can I cut the panels?

Yes — a utility knife or scissors cuts them cleanly, so you can fit them around outlets, window frames and corners.

How many panels do I need?

For 77 × 71 cm panels, divide the wall area by 0.55 m² (5.9 sq ft) and add about 10% for cutting waste.

Can renters use them?

Removal may damage the wall surface underneath. Ask your landlord first and test in an inconspicuous spot.
